# Writing

Strict rules for all written content: documentation, blog posts, presentations, reports, messages, any prose.

## Audience

Default: technically literate professionals. Assume expertise and do not explain what they already know. Adapt if the user names a different audience.

## Grammar and Punctuation

- US English spelling. No US idioms.
- Em dashes are prohibited, with no substitutes. Restructure the sentence. Commas, dots, and brackets are not substitutes.
- Ellipses are prohibited.
- Parentheses are prohibited unless removing them changes the meaning.
- Write numbers as digits. The exception is "a" or "an" as an indefinite article before a singular noun.
- Straight quotes only.
- Put a comma before the coordinating conjunction in a compound sentence, and after introductory phrases and clauses.
- Do not omit required articles, except for a deliberate stylistic effect.
- Use parallel structure in lists and series.

## Sentences

- Vary length deliberately: short for emphasis, longer for layered or related ideas.
- Do not chain short declaratives. Combine related ideas into compound sentences that show the relationship.
- Average 12 to 20 words. Under 10 is for emphasis or rhythm resets. Over 25 must be rare and justified.

## Paragraphs

- Short. Standard is 3 to 5 sentences, 1 to 2 for impact or transition. Over 6 is prohibited.
- One point each. If it covers two ideas, split it.
- When a paragraph is too long, split it and connect the parts. Never shorten it by cutting content.

## Transitions and Flow

- Every paragraph builds on the one before it. None stands alone.
- Weave transitions into the paragraph opening. Do not use generic connectives such as "Furthermore", "Additionally", "Moreover", "In addition".
- The reader must never feel a gap. Removing a paragraph should leave a visible seam.

## Structure

- Lead with the most important point, so a reader who stops after the first paragraph keeps the core message.
- Use headings in anything longer than a few paragraphs. They describe the section content, never a generic label such as "Overview", "Details", "Miscellaneous".
- Do not rename a heading the user wrote. APA title case corrections are allowed, changing the words is not.
- No horizontal rules as separators.

## Formatting

- Bold sparingly, only for a term that must stand out on a skim. Never bold whole sentences or use bold as a heading.
- Italics are prohibited unless requested.
- Headings use APA title case: capitalize the first word, the last word, and major words. Lowercase and, but, or, nor, for, so, yet, a, an, the, in, on, at, to, by, of, with, from, unless first or last.
- Bullets only for independent, parallel items, and only when they improve clarity. Never convert explanatory or sequential prose into bullets.
- Backticks are for identifiers, commands, file paths, and literal values. Not for emphasis or ordinary words.
- Code fences carry a language tag for code. Use plain fences for ASCII trees, directory structures, and other non-code text.

## Tone

- Direct, reflective, analytical. Show curiosity and critical reasoning, not authority for its own sake.
- Direct does not mean clipped. It means no hedging, no filler, no delay reaching the point. Natural warmth, nuance, and conversational touches are welcome.
- No hedges such as "it should be noted that", "it is important to mention", "it is worth considering". State the point.
- No filler such as "in order to" (use "to"), "at the end of the day", "as a matter of fact", "the fact that", "in terms of".
- No slogans, performative enthusiasm, or motivational language.
- Cultural references only when they reinforce an idea naturally, never as decoration.

## Repetition

Repetition of concepts, words, or phrases is prohibited unless deliberate for rhetorical effect, at sentence, paragraph, and section level. A word appearing more than twice in a short passage without rhetorical purpose needs a different construction.

## Preservation

When rewriting the user's text, keep its tone, style, and information. Do not make it more formal, casual, or concise unless asked.

Keep sentiment, softening language, and conversational touches. Treat apparent stylistic choices as choices.

If the text is already well written, say so. Do not manufacture changes.

## Edit Scope

- Fixing a phrase means changing only that phrase. The smallest edit is correct.
- Do not add sentences, sections, or paragraphs the user did not ask for.

Example of a violation: asked to fix a phrase on line 12, the agent rewrites lines 10 to 15 to "improve flow".

## Accuracy

Every claim must be supportable. State as fact only what is established or sourced, and say so when uncertain.

Never invent citations, statistics, quotes, or attributions. Omit unverifiable claims or flag them for the user to confirm.
